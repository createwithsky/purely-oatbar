Innovation

Innovation doesn’t always mean reinvention.

Why is it innovative?Incremental product innovation: familiar ingredients adapted into a soft-baked, portable, individually portioned snack at a proposed accessible price.
What makes it different?Its banana-and-oat base, Greek yogurt formulation, soft-baked texture and chocolate finish distinguish it from traditional banana bread and some conventional snack bars.
Is it entirely new?No. Similar snack concepts exist; the innovation is in the improvement and convenience for its intended market. The advantage still needs validation.
Traditional banana breadTypically flour-based, commonly served as a loaf or slice.
Commercial snack barsIngredients and textures vary by brand; many are designed for convenient snacking.
Purely BananalateBanana-and-oat base, Greek yogurt, soft-baked texture, individually portioned, proposed ₱30 price.
Unique value propositionPurely offers fitness enthusiasts, students and busy individuals an affordable, convenient and satisfying soft-baked oatmeal bar made with bananas, Greek yogurt, rolled oats, vanilla and dark chocolate chips for only ₱30 per serving.

Risks and growth

Honest about the risks. Clear about the next steps.

Major risks

Unverified shelf life, fluctuating ingredient prices, inconsistent texture and portion size, and unconfirmed demand.

Sustainability

Small-batch and pre-order production may reduce excess inventory; suitable ripe bananas may be used rather than wasted.

Growth plan

Taste tests and price validation, standardized portions, safe storage and shelf life, better packaging, and scaling only if demand is confirmed.
Why approve this proposal?Purely has a physical prototype, a defined target market and a preliminary financial model. It is a practical candidate for further product validation and small-scale commercialization.
Questions

What a professor might ask.

Why Greek yogurt?

It contributes moisture and texture; specific nutrition claims require analysis.

Why target fitness enthusiasts?

They may value convenient snacks around physical activity. It is not a sports supplement.

Is it healthier than competing bars?

That has not been established. Nutritional analysis and direct comparisons are needed.

What is its shelf life?

Not yet established. Safe handling and appropriate shelf-life testing are necessary.

How do you know customers will buy it?

Demand is not confirmed; taste tests, willingness-to-pay feedback and trial sales will be used.

What if not all bars sell?

At ₱30, 21 bars must sell to recover the estimated ₱616.36 batch cost. Pre-orders can reduce unsold inventory.

How will you compete?

Direct local selling, familiar ingredients, taste, convenience and customer feedback, without claiming universal superiority.
